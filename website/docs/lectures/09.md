# 09. Network Infrastructure

:::tip[Interactive lecture]

Go through this lecture as short chapters: the animations of the slides step by step (frames on the wires, ARP, NAT, DNS, DHCP); simulators of a hub and a switch, of the network mask, of the routing table and of `tcpdump` filters; a demo laptop with `ip`, `ping`, `nmcli`, `ss`, `tcpdump` and missions, a broken laptop to troubleshoot, quizzes and flashcards.

<a className="button button--primary button--lg" href="/courses/09-network/index.html" target="_blank">Open the interactive lecture →</a>

:::

## You will learn

- a short history of the Internet and what **Internet services** are (the Web is one of them)
- what a **network card** and a **MAC address** are
- what an **Ethernet** and a **Wi-Fi** frame look like
- how **hubs** and **switches** connect computers in a local network, and why switches replaced hubs
- how to see the network cards and the Wi-Fi networks around with `nmcli`
- what **IP addresses**, **network masks** and **IP packets** are, and how to test a computer with `ping`
- how **NAT** hides the private addresses of a home behind one public address
- how **routers** connect networks, and how to see them with `traceroute`
- what **ports**, **TCP** and **UDP** are, and how a program gets a port
- what **DNS** is: how a name like `upb.ro` becomes an IP address, and how `host` asks for it
- what a computer needs to reach the Internet: an **IP address and mask**, a **default gateway** and a **DNS server**, and how **DHCP** configures all of these automatically
- what a **home router** does: DHCP, DNS and **NAT**
- how to **capture packets** with `tcpdump` and Wireshark
- what to check, from the bottom up, when there is no Internet
